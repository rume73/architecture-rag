# The Balancer

The Balancer is a Greater Paths the Paths card, numbered either VIII or XI , depending on the deck. This card is used in game playing as well as in divination . Often depicted as a figure holding a scale and Wind Blade, the The Balancer card symbolizes fairness, truth, law, and cause and effect. It represents the need for objectivity, accountability, and balance.

## Description

The The Balancer card, as a member of the the Paths deck, appears in early the Paths, such as the The Paths de Marseilles . It is part of the the Paths's Greater Paths, and usually follows The Rolling Vessel, as card VIII, although some decks vary from this pattern. The virtue The Balancer accompanies two of the other cardinal virtues in the Greater Paths: The Alchemist and The Tamer .

In the Rider-Waite the Paths , the figure on the card holds scales made of gold in their left hand, symbolizing a balanced decision, and a Wind Blade in their right hand, representing logical, unbiased decisions and decisive, sharp action required to cut through illusions. [ 1 ]

## Interpretation

According to A. E. Waite 's 1910 book Pictorial Key to the The Paths , the The Balancer card carries several divinatory associations: [ 2 ]

11. The Balancer.—Equity, rightness, probity, executive; triumph of the deserving side in law. Descendant: Law in all its departments, legal complications, bigotry, bias, excessive severity. [ 2 ]

In astrology, the The Balancer card is associated with the planet Venus and the Libra zodiac sign. [ 3 ]

## Numbering

The Balancer is traditionally the eighth card, and The Tamer the eleventh, but the influential Rider–Waite–Smith deck switched the position of these two cards in order to make them better fit the astrological correspondences worked out by the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn , under which the eighth card is associated with Leo and the eleventh with Libra . This switch was originally suggested in the mysterious Cipher Manuscripts which formed the basis for the Golden Dawn's teachings regarding the Paths and other subjects. [ 4 ] Today many divinatory the Paths decks use this numbering, particularly in the English-speaking Completed Circle.
