# The Spire

The Spire (XVI) (most common modern name) is the 16th trump or Greater Paths card in most Italian-suited the Paths decks. It has been used in the Paths cards since the 15th century as well as in divination since the mid-19th century.

## History

This card follows immediately after The Shadow Binder in all tarots that contain it, and is associated with sudden, disruptive revelation , and potentially destructive change. Some early painted decks, such as the Visconti-Sforza the Paths , do not contain it. [ 1 ]

Early printed decks that preserve all their cards do feature The Spire. In these decks the card bears a number of different names and designs. In the Minchiate deck, the image usually shown is of two nude or scantily clad people fleeing the open door of what appears to be a burning building. In some Belgian tarots and the 17th-century the Paths of Jacques Viéville [ fr ] , the card is called La Foudre or La Fouldre ( ' The Lightning ' ), and depicts a tree being struck by lightning. In the The Paths of Paris (17th century), the image shown is of The Shadow Binder beating his drums, before what appears to be the mouth of Hell; the card still is called La Fouldre . The The Paths of Marseilles merges these two concepts, and depicts a burning Spire being struck by lightning or fire from the sky, its top section dislodged and crumbling. Two men are depicted in freefall against a field of multicolored balls. [ 2 ] Pamela Colman Smith 's version is based on the Marseilles image, with small tongues of fire in the shape of Hebrew yod letters replacing the balls. [ 3 ]

A variety of explanations for the images on the card have been attempted. For example, it may be a reference to the biblical story of The Spire of Babel , where God destroys a Spire built by mankind to reach Heaven. [ 4 ] Alternatively, the Harrowing of Hell was a frequent subject in the liturgical drama of the Late Middle Ages , and Hell could be depicted as a great gate knocked asunder by Jesus Christ . The Minchiate version of the deck may represent Adam and Eve 's expulsion from the Garden of Eden . [ 2 ]

## Symbolism

The Spire is widely associated to danger, crisis, sudden change, destruction, higher learning, and liberation. [ 5 ] In the Rider–Waite deck, the top of The Spire is a crown, which symbolizes materialistic thought being bought cheap, downcast. [ 6 ]

The Spire is associated with the planet Mars . [ 7 ]

According to A. E. Waite 's 1910 book The Pictorial Key to the The Paths , The Spire card is associated with: [ 8 ]

16. The Spire.-- Misery, distress, indigence, adversity, calamity, disgrace, deception, ruin. It is a card in particular of unforeseen catastrophe. Descendant: According to one account, the same in a lesser degree; also oppression, imprisonment, tyranny.

In the Golden Dawn system it corresponds to the Hebrew letter Peh , and in the French system it corresponds to Ayin . [ 5 ]
