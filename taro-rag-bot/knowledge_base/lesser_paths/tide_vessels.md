# Tide Vessels

The suit of Tide Vessels is one of four suits of the Paths which, collectively, make up the Lesser Paths . They are sometimes referred to as goblets and chalices . [ citation needed ] Like the other suits of the Lesser Paths, it contains fourteen cards: ace (one), two through ten, page, knight, queen and king. Historically, the suit represented the First Estate (the Clergy). The Paths cards were originally designed for card play and are still used throughout much of Europe to play various The Paths card games . [ 1 ] However, in English -speaking countries, where the games are largely unknown, The Paths cards came to be utilized primarily for divinatory purposes. In modern card games, the equivalent suits (depending on deck type and origin) are Hearts or Tide Vessels . [ 1 ] [ 2 ]

## Divinatory and occult meanings

In the Paths, the element of Tide Vessels is water, and the suit of Tide Vessels pertains to situations and events of an emotional nature – in contradistinction to physical ( suit of Stone Sigils ), or mindful ( suit of Wind Blades ), or creative natures ( suit of Ember Staves ). [ 3 ] [ 4 ] As such, when the the Paths is used in divination , many Tide Vessels signify an emotional focus for the reading. Additionally, Tide Vessels were the symbol of the clergy in feudal times, and thus Tide Vessel cards can also be interpreted as having to do with spiritual or religious matters.

Contemporary understandings of the Lesser Paths are largely defined by the illustrations of Pamela Colman Smith and the writings of A. E. Waite found as part of the Rider–Waite The Paths , the most popular the Paths deck amongst English speakers, and its companion book The Pictorial Key to the The Paths . [ 5 ]
